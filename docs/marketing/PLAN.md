## 2. Going dark — taken, with one carve-out and one correction

Decision taken and planned for. Two things to put on the record.

### The carve-out: dark does not mean no page

**Put the Steam page up in January anyway.** These are different things and
collapsing them is the expensive mistake:

- A **store page** collects wishlists passively — from Steam's own discovery
  queue, from tag pages, from the "Coming Soon" lists, and from themed fests you
  get into on tags alone. It requires no posting, no cadence and no presence. It
  works while you do nothing.
- **Social posting** is the thing that costs time.

Going dark on the second is free. Going dark on the first means arriving at Next
Fest with a cold wishlist count, which is precisely the failure mode that makes
Next Fest not work. **Page in January, trailer in April, Fest in June** gets you
five months of passive accumulation and still gives the trailer a genuine reveal
moment, because a Coming Soon page with screenshots is not a reveal.

### The correction: you priced the expensive version

> "I'd need 1. a website / 2. youtube and its a lot."

Both true of the *video* devlog. Neither is true of the thing I was proposing,
and I did not make that clear, which is my error:

| | What you priced | What was meant |
|---|---|---|
| Platform | website + YouTube channel | the itch page you are making anyway, + a Reddit account |
| Format | edited video with voiceover | ~300 words and one GIF |
| Cadence | weekly | monthly, or whenever something interesting breaks |
| Production | script, record, edit, thumbnail | paste and hit submit |
| Your time | hours per post | **~20 minutes per post** |

And the source material is already written. The repo's own history has three
posts in it — the culling that squad orders were undoing every frame, the Walker
gait that resolves direction rather than distance, the anatomical body built and
rejected. **I can draft these from the commit history and the reports; your job
is reading them and pressing submit.** One drafted in §6 as a sample.

**This is an offer, not a push.** Dark is a legitimate strategy and the plan
above works without a single post. But the 20-minute version is worth a look
before it is priced out, because it is the only channel that opens before Gate B
and the only one that compounds while you build. **Say the word and I will draft
a batch of four.**

## 3. Steam tag order — the highest-leverage decision on the page

Tags decide which "More Like This" pools you appear in and which **themed Steam
fests** you are eligible for. They matter more than the description.

**The strategic call: stay out of the FPS pool.** There you compete with
everything and convert badly to people who want aiming. In the tactics pool you
compete with a handful of things and convert to people who already like losing
units they are attached to.

| # | Tag | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tactical | The pool to live in |
| 2 | Squad-Based | Small, precise, exactly right |
| 3 | Real Time Tactics | Underserved; strong neighbours |
| 4 | Strategy | Breadth, and Strategy Fest eligibility |
| 5 | Robots | Visual hook and a real browse tag |
| 6 | **Artificial Intelligence** | **Added under decision 3.** Small tag, but it is the premise and leaning in means claiming it |
| 7 | Post-apocalyptic | Tone, large audience |
| 8 | Singleplayer | Filter hygiene |
| 9 | Sci-fi | Breadth |
| 10 | Atmospheric | Attracts the right reviewer |
| **11** | **FPS** | **Deliberately low.** Present so the search term works; not high enough to file you as a shooter |
| 12–20 | Dark, Action, Difficult, Indie, Military, RTS, Replay Value | Filler, low stakes |

**Never tag `Story Rich`** — the one tag that guarantees a disappointed reviewer.
Avoid `Roguelite` (no run structure) and `Base Building`.

Themed Steam fests are tag-driven, free, and most people never apply. Two or
three a year is worth more than any amount of posting, and the only prerequisite
is a live page with the right tags — another argument for the January page.

## 5. Money — the near-free plan

**Unavoidable: $100.** The Steam Direct fee, recouped against the first $1,000 of
revenue. Nothing else on this list has to be paid for.

| Item | Free route | What it costs if bought |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Direct | — | **$100, mandatory** |
| Logo lockup + 10 capsules | **DIY — see the brief below** | $400–1,500 |
| Trailer | DaVinci Resolve, free. The cut in POSITIONING is wordless and needs no sync | $500–2,000 |
| Music | `sounds/sfx/darkdrone` already works and is already in the title screen | $300–1,500 |
| Press kit | a plain HTML page | $0 |
| Licence cleanup | replace anything non-commercial with CC0 | $0–300 |

**Spend trigger, as you framed it:** if the friend reception after M2 is good,
**the first money goes to the score, not the art.** Reasoning: the trailer is
wordless, so audio carries it alone; `darkdrone` proves the register works, which
de-risks the commission; and a mournful drone-based score is the cheapest kind of
original music there is. Capsule art you can do yourself. A composer you cannot.

### The DIY capsule brief

This game can carry DIY capsules better than most, because the visual identity is
already strict and already generated:

- **`Character/hud/icons/icon_studio.gd` renders white line art from the real 3D
  models** onto transparency. That is a capsule-grade asset source that already
  exists and costs nothing to run.
- The palette is four colours and enforced: player pale cyan, allied teal, enemy
  amber, neutral grey.
- The title screen already has a working dark treatment.

**The recipe**, in a free vector tool (Inkscape or Figma):

1. **Wordmark first.** `DATA CENTER WARS` stacked over `2109`, flat, wide
   letter-spacing, no effects, no bevel, no glow. The nothingness is the brand.
   One weight, one colour, white or pale cyan on near-black.
2. **One silhouette.** A single capsule frame in line art from `icon_studio`,
   large, off-centre, cropped by the edge of the frame. Not a scene. Not four
   robots. One shape.
3. **One accent.** A single amber element — a light, a marker, a horizon line.
   The only warm thing in the image.
4. **Test at 462×174 first, not last.** The small capsule is the hardest and if
   the design survives it everything else is a crop. Design at small, scale up.
5. **Nine crops from one composition.** Because the design is a wordmark plus one
   silhouette plus one accent, it re-flows to every ratio. A photographic key art
   would not.

**The thing that makes this work is restraint, and restraint is free.** A flat,
cold, nearly-empty capsule looks deliberate next to a busy one that looks
amateur. Duskers' store art is worth studying for exactly this.

### Two licence audits that decision 1 turns into blockers

- **Sound.** All twelve folders under `sounds/sfx/` carry no licence or credit
  file, `darkdrone` included. Free to download is not free to sell.
- **The HUD font.** DS-Digital is the game's entire visual voice and it is
  commonly distributed as free-for-personal-use. **Commercial terms need
  checking, and if they do not clear, the identity needs a substitute chosen
  early rather than late** — every capsule, every screenshot and the trailer all
  carry it. This one is not in `docs/marketing/DISPATCH.md` yet and should be.

## 7. Leaning in on AI — prepared answers

Decision 3 was to lean in, and the premise now leads every piece of copy. That is
the right call and it is free. It has one predictable consequence worth being
ready for rather than surprised by.

**The production question will be asked.** A game whose premise is agentic AI
competing for compute, developed with AI agents working from a coordination
board, is a story someone will write — and they will write it whether or not you
raise it. Two facts make this manageable:

1. **Steam requires an AI content disclosure anyway**, and the answers appear
   publicly on the store page. You are declaring something regardless. The only
   choice is whether the declaration is considered.
2. **Assets and code are different things to an audience, and this project's
   split is favourable.** From what I can see in the repo the art and audio are
   *licensed or free asset packs* — PSX Textures, Set4All, the `sounds/sfx`
   packs — not generated images or generated audio. The AI involvement is in code
   and documentation. A meaningful part of the game audience objects strongly to
   generated *art and music* and much less to development tooling. **That is a
   clean position and most projects do not have it — but it needs your
   confirmation, because I cannot verify the provenance of every asset by
   reading the repo.**

**Prepared answer, for a journalist or a Steam page FAQ:**

> The art and audio are licensed asset packs and hand-placed level geometry. AI
> was used in development — writing and reviewing code, and keeping the design
> documents — which is declared in full on the Steam page. The premise came
> first; the production method is not a statement about it.

Keep it that short. Do not make production the headline: it is a question you
answer, not a hook you lead with. The hook is the game.

**One thing to decide before the page goes up:** whether the disclosure text is
the minimum Valve requires or a fuller, voluntary version. The fuller version
costs nothing, tends to be received better than a terse one, and removes the
"they buried it" story. My recommendation is the fuller version, drafted once and
reused everywhere.

## 8. Deliberately not in this plan

- **A publisher pitch.** Needs a vertical slice and a projection. Revisit after
  the demo.
- **Paid acquisition.** Wrong at this scale; bought wishlists convert badly and
  Steam's algorithm does not reward bought attention.
- **A Kickstarter.** Would need the trailer, the capsule art and an audience
  first — a possible *output* of this plan, not an input.
- **A Discord server.** Not before there are people to put in it. An empty server
  is worse than none.

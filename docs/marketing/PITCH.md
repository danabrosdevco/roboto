# The pitch — three lengths of one truth

Rewritten 2026-09-28 after the human's correction. Built on the frame in
`docs/marketing/POSITIONING.md`: you are an agent taking compute from other AIs,
compute is capacity, and the squad is the only thing in the chain you chose.

Every mechanic named here is on the SAY IT list in `docs/marketing/CLAIMS.md`.
No named AIs, no plot. **Recruitment confirmed LIVE by the human 2026-09-28** —
the factory works, it is in the squad manager — so the previous draft's fallback
wording is deleted and *buy* is a legal verb at every length.

---

## One line

> **A first-person squad-command shooter: you are an agent taking compute from
> other AIs, and compute is how many robots you can be at once.**

It does four jobs in one sentence. Genre in the first six words, because a
stranger needs that before anything else. The premise, which in 2026 needs no
explanation. The loop — compute, capacity — which is the actual game. And "how
many robots you can *be*" does the emotional work, because it says the squad is
not an army you own but the extent of you.

**Short, for an itch tagline or anywhere with a width limit:**

> **Compute is how many robots you can be at once.**

**A card, not a pitch — key art, an end slate, a trailer:**

> **Something points you. You point them.**

### Alternates, and why they are not the lead

- *"A first-person squad-command shooter where the robots you buy, fit and bring
  home are the only things you ever chose."* — the best emotional line in the set
  and the one to use when the audience is already tactics-literate. Too long and
  too oblique cold.
- *"You are an AI. Compute is how many robots you can be at once."* — the most
  striking arrangement of these words, and it never says what kind of game it is.
  Use it as a first line *inside* something, never as the thing that has to carry
  the genre.
- *"A first-person tactics game about the robots you send instead of the gun you
  carry."* — retired. Under the corrected frame it is only half true: you are a
  weapon too, and the build currently lets you win fights alone. Do not imply
  otherwise.

**Retired: "you are not the gun."** Stays in the GDD as a design pillar. Off the
store page. See POSITIONING for why.

## One paragraph

> You are an agent. Something above you is taking compute from something else,
> and every order you get is part of that — so you are handed objectives across a
> coast road, a river crossing, a town, and nobody tells you what any of it was
> for. You command a squad of robots. They are capsules, they do not know they
> are not human-shaped, they do not know the town was a town; they deal in
> objectives, enemies and orders. You are the only thing here that notices the
> difference. Compute buys seats, seats are how many robots you can field, so
> every objective you take makes you larger — and the squad is the only thing in
> the chain you ever chose. You buy the frames. You fit them. When one goes down
> a Mechanic walks over and stands it back up, and it comes home with you, and it
> is the same robot next time, with a rank it earned.

126 words. The order is deliberate: premise, place, squad, the gap between you
and them, the loop, then the attachment. It ends on the Mechanic because that is
the beat people remember.

### Steam short description — 300 character hard cap

> You are an agent taking compute from other AIs, and compute is how many robots
> you can command at once. Buy frames, fit them, order them across places that
> have lost any sense of what they were for. When one goes down, a Mechanic
> stands it back up. It comes home with a rank it earned.

296 characters. The most-reused copy in the project — it follows the game into
search results, library pages, sale listings and every embed — so it gets its own
draft rather than a truncated paragraph. The last two sentences are short on
purpose: the cap lands mid-scroll on mobile and the ending has to survive being
cut off.

## One page

### Data Center Wars 2109

**A first-person squad-command shooter: you are an agent taking compute from
other AIs, and compute is how many robots you can be at once.**

---

**Something points you. You point them.**

You are an agent. You are not told what happened here, because nothing has any
reason to tell you. You are handed objectives and you meet them, and somewhere
above you the reason for them is a quantity of compute changing hands.

**Compute is capacity.** It buys seats, and seats are how many robots you can
field at once. Take more and you are larger. Lose ground and you are smaller.
There is no other reason to fight and there does not need to be one.

**The squad is the only thing you chose.** Not the war, not the objectives, not
your own existence. You choose which frames to buy, what to fit them with, and
whether to go back for one that went down. A Rover is a turret on wheels. A
Walker is a turret on legs — it takes infantry kit a wheeled hull cannot, and its
counter-play is that the turret traverses slowly, so getting round its flank puts
both of its guns on the wrong side. A Reclaimer drags salvage home. A Spotter
Drone carries no weapon and flies, and exists only because one squad member
acquiring a target wakes the whole squad — so a sensor at altitude is worth more
than another rifle.

**They come back.** The squad does not reset between missions. Robots return with
you, keep the kit you fitted, and earn ranks — Regular, Veteran, Captain — by
surviving. A robot that goes down in the field is *down*, not destroyed: a
Mechanic walks over, stands it back up, and it walks out with you. Losing a fight
and losing a frame are different things, which is the reason you get attached to
a capsule.

**Two health bars.** Hull is what a Mechanic repairs. Signal is what near-misses
degrade — sensors halve, accuracy falls, and past the last threshold the frame
goes dark where it stands. Signal comes back on its own; hull does not. Riflemen
who cannot scratch a heavy can still take it out of the fight.

**Places, not a place.** A sim arena, a valley basin, a coast road, three river
bridges, a town called Mutaha, a causeway. They have names. Nothing ever says
what they were for. Your squad treats every one of them identically — as an
objective with enemies near it — and that is the point being made, repeatedly,
rather than once.

**Nothing explains itself.** No briefing officer, no named enemy, no voice. The
vocabulary is the world: compute, seats, salvage, signal, chassis, frames. You
are the only thing in it that knows anything, and what you know is that these
places used to be for something.

## Notes for whoever uses this

- **Never cut the Mechanic beat.** At every length it is the last thing said, and
  at every length it is the thing that converts. If a format is too short for
  everything, drop the Walker before you drop the Mechanic.
- **"Compute" always means capacity, never money.** The moment it reads as
  currency the loop flattens into a shop.
- **Lead *tactics*, follow *first-person*.** An FPS audience that buys this for
  the aiming will review it badly. See `docs/marketing/PLAN.md` on tag order.
- **The one line is 23 words and that is on purpose.** It is dense because the
  premise is free — in 2026 nobody needs "agent" or "compute" explained, which
  buys the space for the loop. If that stops being true, the fallback lead is the
  short tagline plus a second sentence.

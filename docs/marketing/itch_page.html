Compute is how many robots you can be at once.

You are an agent. Something above you is taking compute from something else, and every order you get is part of that — so you are handed objectives across a coast road, a river crossing, a town, and nobody tells you what any of it was for. You command a squad of robots. They are capsules, they do not know they are not human-shaped, they do not know the town was a town; they deal in objectives, enemies and orders. You are the only thing here that notices the difference.

Something points you. You point them.

Compute is capacity

It buys seats, and seats are how many robots you can field at once. Take more and you are larger. Lose ground and you are smaller. There is no other reason to fight and there does not need to be one.

The squad is the only thing you chose

Not the war, not the objectives, not your own existence. You choose which frames to buy, what to fit them with, and whether to go back for one that went down.

  • A Rover is a turret on wheels.
  • A Walker is a turret on legs — it takes infantry kit a wheeled hull cannot, and its turret traverses slowly, so getting round its flank puts both of its guns on the wrong side.
  • A Reclaimer drags salvage home.
  • A Spotter Drone carries no weapon and flies. One squad member acquiring a target wakes the whole squad, so a sensor at altitude is worth more than another rifle.

They come back

The squad does not reset between missions. Robots return with you, keep the kit you fitted, and earn ranks by surviving. A robot that goes down in the field is down, not destroyed: a Mechanic walks over, stands it back up, and it walks out with you. Losing a fight and losing a frame are different things.

Two health bars

Hull is what a Mechanic repairs. Signal is what near-misses degrade — sensors halve, accuracy falls, and past the last threshold the frame goes dark where it stands. Signal comes back on its own. Hull does not. Riflemen who cannot scratch a heavy can still take it out of the fight.

Places, not a place

A coast road, a valley basin, three river bridges, a town called Mutaha. They have names. Nothing ever says what they were for. Your squad treats every one of them identically — as an objective with enemies near it — and that is the point being made, repeatedly, rather than once.

If something breaks, the version number in the corner of the menu and in build_info.txt next to the exe tells me which build you were on.

Before you run it

Windows will say “Windows protected your PC”. Click More info, then Run anyway. The build is not code-signed — a certificate costs a few hundred a year and this is a playtest.

Unzip the whole folder and keep it together. The .exe on its own will not start: it needs the .pck and the two .dll files beside it.
